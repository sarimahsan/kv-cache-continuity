### 1. What We Can Run on Colab (Feasible & High-Impact)

#### A. Sequence Length & Evaluation Size (Zero Training — Takes ~15–20 min on Colab)
* **The Vulnerability:** Primary tables evaluate only $N=6$ sequences ($T=64$, ~378 tokens) on WikiText-2. Reviewers will call this a toy sample.
* **The Fix:**
  1. **Scale $N$ and $T$:** Expand the evaluation to $N=50$ or $100$ sequences and test a length sweep: $T \in \{64, 128, 256, 512\}$.
  2. **Add a Second Dataset:** Run Native Projection on 50–100 sequences from an out-of-domain dataset (e.g., **C4 validation** or **The Stack / Python code**).
* **Why it's feasible:** Native Projection is pure inference with frozen weights. Evaluating 100 sequences at $T=256$ takes **less than 10 minutes** on a standard Colab GPU (T4/L4/A100).
* **Impact on Paper:** Immediately knocks out the objection that Native Projection is an artifact of short, 64-token Wikipedia snippets.

#### B. Multi-Depth Adapter Characterization (Takes ~20–30 min on Colab)
* **The Vulnerability:** You only trained adapters at $L=18$ (50% depth on Qwen). A reviewer will argue: *"Of course an MLP adapter struggles to bridge 18 missing layers (50% depth). What happens at 67% ($L=24$) or 75% ($L=27$)? Is intermediate readout hard everywhere, or just at 50%?"*

  - Train the existing bottleneck adapter ($r=512$ or $r=1024$) at **two additional depths**: e.g., $L=24$ (67%) and $L=28$ (78%).
* **Why it's feasible:** Training a 2M-parameter adapter for 1,500–2,000 steps on Colab takes **~10–15 minutes per depth**.
* **Impact on Paper:** Transforms your result from *"a one-off negative result at layer 18"* into a **depth-scaling curve of readout recoverability**, showing at what depth an adapter becomes viable.

#### C. A Third Model Family (Takes ~15 min on Colab)
